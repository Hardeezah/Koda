# NIGERIA CUSTOMS SERVICE (NCS) - STANDARD IMPORT DOCUMENTATION AND PROCEDURES

## 1. Introduction
The Nigeria Customs Service (NCS) is the primary government agency responsible for the collection of customs revenue, facilitation of national and international trade, and anti-smuggling activities. All goods imported into Nigeria must pass through NCS protocols and the Nigeria Single Window Trade Portal.

## 2. Core Import Documents
Before initiating any importation, the importer or their authorized customs clearing agent must ensure the following documentation is accurately prepared:

1. **Form M:** The foundational document for all imports into Nigeria. It must be processed through an Authorized Dealer Bank (ADB). Form M declares the importer's intent to bring goods into the country and facilitates foreign exchange transactions through the Central Bank of Nigeria (CBN).
2. **Pre-Arrival Assessment Report (PAAR):** Generated via the Nigeria Single Window Trade Portal after Form M has been validated. The PAAR calculates the baseline customs duties, taxes, and risk profile of the cargo before it arrives at the port.
3. **Bill of Lading / Airway Bill:** The official shipping document issued by the carrier, detailing the type, quantity, and destination of the goods.
4. **Commercial Invoice & Packing List:** Specifies the transaction value, seller, buyer, and the detailed contents and packaging of the shipment.
5. **Combined Certificate of Value and Origin (CCVO):** Required to authenticate the value of the goods and their country of origin.
6. **Marine Insurance Certificate:** Importers must obtain insurance for their cargo from a Nigerian insurance company.

## 3. Agency Permits and Certificates
Depending on the Harmonized System (HS) code and the nature of the goods, supplementary certificates from other regulatory bodies must be attached to the Customs declaration:
- **SONCAP Certificate:** From the Standards Organisation of Nigeria (SON) for manufactured goods.
- **NAFDAC Permit:** For food, drugs, chemicals, cosmetics, and medical devices.
- **NESREA Clearance:** For environmental, electronic, and hazardous materials.

## 4. The Clearance Process (NICIS II)
The NCS utilizes the Nigeria Integrated Customs Information System (NICIS II) for processing imports.
1. The importer's agent submits the Single Goods Declaration (SGD) electronically through NICIS II.
2. The PAAR, Commercial Invoice, and Bill of Lading are attached.
3. The system assesses the final payable Customs Duty, 7.5% VAT, 7% Port Surcharge, 0.5% ECOWAS Levy, and 1% Comprehensive Import Supervision Scheme (CISS) administrative charge.
4. Payment is made to designated government accounts.
5. The cargo is subjected to physical examination or non-intrusive scanning by Customs officers. If no discrepancies are found between the physical cargo and the SGD/PAAR, the goods are released.

## 5. ECOWAS Common External Tariff (CET)
Nigeria applies the ECOWAS Common External Tariff (CET) for imports from non-ECOWAS countries. The tariff bands are generally structured as:
- 0% for essential social goods.
- 5% for basic raw materials and capital goods.
- 10% for intermediate goods.
- 20% for finished consumer goods.
- 35% for specific goods for economic protection.
Importers must ensure correct HS Code classification to determine the appropriate CET band.
